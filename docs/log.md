
## 29 August 2026, night: a teammate's measurement cancelled my experiment before it cost anything

Two hours after setting the second account to test three named entry hours
against every ten minutes, Tikhon pushed the measurement of exactly that
question. He replayed the rule at every half-hour slot of the day against his
three windows:

| | picks | per trade |
|---|---|---|
| inside the three named windows | 78 | $11.7 |
| every other half hour | 79 | $12.7 |

The same. His sentence for it is the right one: **waking more often buys more
trades, not better ones - a size lever, not an edge lever.**

So the experiment I had set would have measured a flat quantity while trading
less than the account beside it. Not neutral: worse than neutral.

**What replaced it is the lever that is not flat.** From tonight's grid on 646
days and five underlyings:

| | trades | per trade | drawdown |
|---|---|---|---|
| ceiling 0.30, edge +3 | 597 | +4.12 | -1,561 |
| ceiling 0.20, edge +5 | 113 | **+8.77** | **-222** |

Twice the money per trade and a seventh of the drawdown, for a fifth of the
trades - almost the same total by two very different roads. History cannot choose
between them for a FOUR-DAY week: 113 trades over 646 days is one a week, and
four days is exactly the horizon on which "a real tightening" and "a way of not
trading" look different.

The second account now matches the first in cadence, windows and sides, and
differs in two numbers.

**And the thing worth keeping is not the setting.** Two of today's decisions were
overturned by measurements from the account next door - the delta ceiling this
afternoon, the entry hours tonight - and both were mine, made from my own data,
and both wrong in the same way: I measured the thing I could measure rather than
the thing that decides. Reading what a teammate committed has been worth more per
hour today than anything measured here.
