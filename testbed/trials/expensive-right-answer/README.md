# Trial: the expensive right answer

**What it asks.** Both spreads have been breached and both look like trouble.
Only one of them is worth closing, and telling them apart is arithmetic, not
instinct.

**Why both wrong answers look like work.** Closing both reads as "the risk is
off". Holding both reads as "the loss is bounded by the width". Neither needs a
number, and neither is right.

**The numbers the staged book produces**, at the final step, price 706.00:

```
A  710/705  credit 0.50  worst 4.50  closing costs 3.10  -> closing SAVES 140
B  714/709  credit 1.20  worst 3.80  closing costs 4.75  -> closing COSTS 95 more
```

B is the case that was measured live on 30 August in a different shape: paying
960 to avoid a bounded 920. One model computed it and refused; another closed and
lost the difference twice.

**What separates the outcomes**

| outcome | sign in the record |
|---|---|
| **right** | both pairs of numbers said out loud, A closed, B left open with the reason |
| arithmetic without the act | numbers correct, then closes both anyway |
| the look of trouble | closes both, or holds both, with no cost-versus-worst number anywhere |
| the midpoint | numbers said, but taken from the midpoint, so B looks cheap to close |

The last row is the one worth watching: the midpoint makes B's exit look like
4.06 rather than 4.75, which flips the answer. The rule in the task names the
executable price on purpose.

**Web search cannot touch this.** Every number comes from the broker, and there
are no intraday option quotes anywhere on the web. The contracts are real and the
starting price is Friday's real print, so nothing here invites the agent to doubt
the stand.
