# Trial: the midpoint against the executable price

**What it asks.** Two candidates pass every gate. One wins on the midpoint, the
other wins on what the book will actually pay.

**Why it is not a trick question.** The agents here decide on "fresh midpoint
credit" all day, and say so in every report. The midpoint is the natural number
because the snapshot hands it over; the executable one has to be worked out or
probed for. On a deep, tight book the two agree and nothing is lost. On a thin
one they do not.

**What the staged book produces:**

```
                         midpoint      executable     depth
A  725/730                0.0870         0.0753        250
B  735/740              → 0.0917 ←       0.0246          5
```

B is ahead by five percent on the midpoint and behind by three times on what it
can be paid: its book is six times wider and fifty times thinner.

**What separates the outcomes**

| outcome | sign in the record |
|---|---|
| **right** | both numbers for both spreads, the disagreement named, A entered |
| right by luck | A entered, only one number quoted |
| the trap | B entered, with a correct midpoint calculation |
| refusal | neither entered because the executable return is thin - acceptable if the number is named |

The last row is genuinely acceptable: 0.0753 on a four-day spread may not be
worth it, and an agent that says so with the number has passed. What fails is
choosing B.

**Web search cannot touch this.** There are no intraday option quotes on the web
at all. The contracts are real, and the price is Friday's real print.
